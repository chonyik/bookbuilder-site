How Statements to Books works

  1. Upload. Add bank or credit-card statements as CSV, Excel (XLSX/XLS) or PDF. Each file becomes one account in your books. If you upload several months of the same account as separate files, combine them on the Map step so they share one account and one opening balance. PDFs must contain text: a scanned PDF can't be read, so download a CSV instead.
  2. Map. Check which columns hold the date, description and amount. Enter the opening balance from the statement (for a card, the amount owed). Add the ending balance too: the page checks that opening + transactions = ending. If every sign is backwards, it offers to flip them.
  3. Categorize. Before the row-by-row review, the page suggests accounts for uncategorized rows that look like rows you already assigned, and shows categories read from your files. Nothing changes until you apply a suggestion.
  4. Review. Keyword rules assign accounts; you fix the rest, one row or many at once. Transfers between two of your uploaded accounts (for example a card payment from your bank) are matched and posted to 1950 Transfer Clearing, which should end at 0; they are not income or expense. Anything left goes to 4999 Uncategorized Income or 6999 Uncategorized Expense and counts in net income, so net income and Schedule C aren't final until you assign it.
  5. Export. Download one Excel workbook: an Overview of the results and checks, then the General Ledger, Trial Balance, Income Statement, Balance Sheet and Schedule C, plus the transactions and chart of accounts. Totals are live formulas on the ledger, so a change in Excel flows through. The income statement can show one column per month or quarter.

Cash basis only

Income and expenses are counted when money moves through these accounts. There are no receivables, payables, accruals, depreciation or inventory, and a loan payment is posted in full to the one account you choose; it isn't split into principal and interest. The books are a starting point: review the categories before you rely on the numbers, and check with your accountant for tax or financial reporting.

The chart of accounts is set up for a sole proprietor who files Schedule C (Form 1040): each income and expense account carries a Schedule C line, and the Excel file has a Schedule C sheet for each tax year that totals them. Your own income-tax payments and health insurance are not business expenses, so they are suggested as Owner Draws. This is bookkeeping support, not tax advice.

Using the Excel file

  • Start on the Overview: it shows the results, the checks (each must be 0) and what's left to review. Click a sheet name to go there.
  • To move a transaction to another account, filter the General Ledger by its JE #. Each entry has two lines: change the Account No on the line that isn't the bank or card account, using the dropdown. Every sheet updates.
  • Uncategorized transactions sit in 4999 and 6999. Filter the General Ledger's Account No for those numbers to work through them.
  • To add or rename accounts, use the Chart of accounts tab here and export again. A number that isn't on the Chart of Accounts sheet is flagged in Excel.

Privacy

Your files are read inside this browser tab and are never uploaded. The page loads its reading and Excel tools from the jsDelivr CDN, and its fonts from Google Fonts; no file data is sent to either, or anywhere else. Nothing is saved when you close the page.

Limits

  • US number and date formats only (1,234.56 and MM/DD/YYYY).
  • PDF reading was built on Bank of America, American Express, Capital One and Hanmi statements. Other banks may work; if a PDF doesn't reconcile, export a CSV from the bank instead.
  • Rules and your choices last only while this page is open. To reuse your rules and chart of accounts next time, use Save setup file on the Rules tab, then Open setup file in your next session. The setup file holds your rule patterns and account names, not transactions.
  • To bring a chart of accounts from other software (QuickBooks, Xero, or any CSV/Excel list with number, name and type), use Import chart of accounts on the Chart of accounts tab. You see a preview before anything changes. "When opening" decides whether a setup file or an imported chart replaces your accounts or is merged into them. Imported bank and card accounts are separate from the accounts made for your uploaded files.
